⚠ Educational teaching tool — not for clinical use

EgyDose is a free learning aid for students of radiotherapy physics and dosimetry, created by Hosny Hassan Mohamed Farag-Allah (Version 8.1, 2026). It is not a medical device, is not validated or commissioned for any machine, and must never be used to plan, check or deliver treatment to real patients.

Independence

This is an independent project. It is not affiliated with, sponsored by or endorsed by any treatment-planning-system or accelerator manufacturer. No vendor software, source code, documentation text or measured beam data were used: the physics follows published textbook methods and the beam data are representative, invented teaching values. Any product or company names familiar to users are trademarks of their respective owners; the screen layout is a generic teaching layout.

What it is

  • Synthetic parametric anatomical phantoms (not patient data; no patient CTs were used), with CT and simulated MR (T1, T2, T1+Gd) and a rigid CT–MR registration exercise. Shapes follow textbook anatomy and published contouring guidance (e.g. the RTOG GU pelvic-node consensus: 7 mm around the iliac vessels, excluding bone, muscle, bowel and bladder); they are idealised, so real patients will look different.
  • A simplified kernel dose engine: primary + scatter Gaussian kernels, TMR from representative 10×10 PDD tables, radiological-depth density correction, an approximate lateral-electron-transport model in low density (penumbra broadening plus a wide electron tail, so small fields in lung lose dose; calibrated to one Monte Carlo lung-slab benchmark) with a scatter reduction in low-density surroundings, MLC transmission, a representative wedge model, an optional couch top, and a setup-error robustness approximation (a shifted copy of the dose, not a recalculation).
  • Dose in the beam's-eye view (button in the BEV): isodose wash and lines on the plane perpendicular to the beam at a chosen depth, or the maximum along each ray, updated live while you edit leaves and jaws; field-in-field sub-fields (own aperture and weight; the MU reference is the parent field) with an Auto-block that closes leaves over PTV hot spots and keeps the change only if the recalculated PTV dose error improves.
  • Plan optimisation (IMRT sliding-window, VMAT) with simplified objectives, for learning how objectives, normalisation and DVHs interact.
  • Learn & Save tab: guided exercises with a live checklist and score, goals / benchmark table, gamma-index and dose-profile tools, save of the plan as a one-page summary image (PNG, loadable again) or .json, printable report. Plan Sum and Radiobiology (BED, EQD2, LKB NTCP, Poisson TCP) tabs in External Beam Planning.
  • Five AAPM TG-119-style benchmark cases in a water cylinder (multi-target, mock prostate, mock head & neck, C-shape easy/hard). The geometry is reconstructed from published descriptions, not the original phantom.
  • Import DICOM: your own anonymised CT slices + RTSTRUCT, processed only in this browser (uncompressed DICOM; tested on synthetic files).

Known limitations (what it does not model)

  • Not a convolution-superposition or Monte Carlo algorithm: no true electron transport (loss of lateral electronic equilibrium in lung is only approximated, calibrated to a central-axis Monte Carlo benchmark only, and not valid for very small fields or at lung boundaries), no accurate small-field (<3 cm) output, no PDD change with field size beyond a simple scatter term, no beam hardening in wedges, no off-axis horns/softening, no dosimetric leaf gap, a simplified couch (tick only if the CT has none) and no immobilisation. External comparisons are limited to that benchmark and one real plan (see VALIDATION.md); they are not a commissioning.
  • Default dose grid is 5 mm: small structures (optic nerve, cochlea, lens) have only a handful of voxels, so their DVH values are coarse.
  • Wedge, MLC, output and penumbra values are representative textbook-level numbers, not measured beam data. The MR is synthetic and its mis-registration is simulated.

Self-checks that were run

  • Water phantom, 10×10 at SSD 100 cm: dose at dmax = 1.00 cGy/MU; depth–dose reproduces the input table to ≲1 % for 6/10/15 MV; 20–80 % penumbra ≈ 6.5 mm; field edge at the geometric 50 % position.
  • Output factor vs field size follows typical 6 MV trends. Wedge isodose tilt at 10 cm depth reproduces the nominal angle within ~3° (10×10 field); MU ratio wedge/open equals 1/wedge factor. In a water–lung–water slab the central-axis dose in lung falls (relative to simple depth scaling) by about 25 % (3×3 cm), 13 % (5×5 cm) and 1 % (10×10 cm) at 6 MV, and more at 10 and 15 MV, as published for lung of density ≈ 0.25.

Suggested learning exercises
  1. Wedge: pelvis case → wedge the two lateral fields (15°→60°), choose dose display “Selected field only”, and watch the isodose tilt toward the toe. Compare MU with/without wedge against 1/wedge factor.
  2. Heterogeneity: lung case → switch Heterogeneity on/off and recalculate; explain the change in PTV D95 and MU.
  3. Collimator margin: “Fit MLC to PTV” with 0, 5 and 8 mm margins, then edit leaves in the BEV; relate PTV coverage to OAR dose.
  4. 3D-CRT vs IMRT vs VMAT: pelvis → compare CI, HI, rectum V50/V70, bladder V50, MU and DVHs.
  5. Normalisation: compare “100% at isocentre” with “PTV D95 = 100%”; discuss hot spots and what each convention hides.
  6. Fusion: brain or femur → Image Registration, judge the mis-registration with Split/Checkerboard, run auto-registration and read the NMI; compare lesions on CT vs T1+Gd.

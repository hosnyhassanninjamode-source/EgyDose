⚠ Educational teaching tool — not for clinical use

EgyDose is a free learning aid for students of radiotherapy physics and dosimetry, created by Hosny (2026). It is not a medical device, is not validated or commissioned for any machine, and must never be used to plan, check or deliver treatment to real patients.

Independence

This is an independent project. It is not affiliated with, sponsored by or endorsed by any treatment-planning-system or accelerator manufacturer. No vendor software, source code, documentation text or measured beam data were used: the physics follows published textbook methods and the beam data are representative, invented teaching values. Any product or company names familiar to users are trademarks of their respective owners; the screen layout is a generic teaching layout.

What it is

  • Synthetic parametric anatomical phantoms (not patient data), with CT and simulated MR (T1, T2, T1+Gd) and a rigid CT–MR registration exercise.
  • A simplified kernel dose engine: primary + scatter Gaussian kernels, TMR from representative 10×10 PDD tables, radiological-depth density correction, simple lateral scaling in low density, MLC transmission, a representative wedge model.
  • Plan optimisation (IMRT sliding-window, VMAT) with simplified objectives, for learning how objectives, normalisation and DVHs interact.

Known limitations (what it does not model)

  • Not a convolution-superposition or Monte Carlo algorithm: no electron transport, no loss of lateral electronic equilibrium in lung, no accurate small-field (<3 cm) output, no PDD change with field size beyond a simple scatter term, no beam hardening in wedges, no off-axis horns/softening, no couch or immobilisation.
  • Default dose grid is 5 mm: small structures (optic nerve, cochlea, lens) have only a handful of voxels, so their DVH values are coarse.
  • Wedge, MLC, output and penumbra values are representative textbook-level numbers, not measured beam data. The MR is synthetic and its mis-registration is simulated.

Self-checks that were run

  • Water phantom, 10×10 at SSD 100 cm: dose at dmax = 1.00 cGy/MU; depth–dose reproduces the input table to ≲1 % for 6/10/15 MV; 20–80 % penumbra ≈ 6.5 mm; field edge at the geometric 50 % position.
  • Output factor vs field size follows typical 6 MV trends. Wedge isodose tilt at 10 cm depth reproduces the nominal angle within ~3° (10×10 field); MU ratio wedge/open equals 1/wedge factor. Low-density slab behaves as expected for a depth-scaling algorithm.
